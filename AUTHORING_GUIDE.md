# Learner-facing activity guide

Use these rules whenever adding or revising the current or a future lesson.

## Writing every activity

- Use a plausible daily-life, school, or work situation: a message, plan, purchase, short conversation, practical choice, or everyday task.
- Apply this standard to every activity type: vocabulary, reading, listening, matching, gap-fill, dialogue or simulation, true/false, and every assessment.
- Make each task useful and precise enough to have one defensible answer. Supply enough context to rule out other options; revise or remove anything ambiguous.
- Avoid artificial “grammar for grammar’s sake” wording. Do not ask learners to name, define, explain, or identify a grammar rule in a question.
- Keep a grammar explanation out of the prompt. After a correct grammar answer, give a short explanation of why it fits that situation and name the relevant rule there.
- Replace true/false rule claims with a clear contextual choice, sentence, dialogue, or other practical format whenever possible.

## Quality and privacy check

- Review every learner-facing prompt, option, hint, result, and feedback message across every exercise type—not only a final test.
- Keep the established page structure and visual design unless a lesson needs an accessible improvement.
- Use no personal details, meeting records, transcripts, contact details, or other student data in published content.
